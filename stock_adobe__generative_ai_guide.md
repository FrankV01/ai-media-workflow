# 10 Tips for Getting Your Generative AI Images Approved for Sale on Adobe Stock

## 1. Know Your Rights

Review the terms and conditions for the software you’re using to create generative AI content. Make sure you have the rights to license your content for commercial purposes. The Adobe Stock team can’t give advice or support for third-party applications, including generative AI software, but we can provide advice and support for Adobe Firefly.

## 2. Don’t Infringe on the Rights of Other Creatives

Don’t include “in the style of” in your prompts or use visual or written prompts based on real people, fictional characters, or the work of other artists. Doing so may infringe on their third-party IP or personality rights.

## 3. Carefully Consider Your Prompts

Use unique prompts to create generative AI assets. Once you envision what you want to create, be as thorough and descriptive as possible. Keep each element of the final output in mind.

- Who or what is in the image?
- What are they doing?
- Where are they?
- What type of lighting is in the image?
- Is it a photorealistic image?

Consider and describe all elements that you expect to see in your generated image. If you’re new to generative AI prompting, be sure to experiment and learn from others before submitting images to Adobe Stock. Check out resources on YouTube for tips to create good prompts.

## 4. Be Selective

Just because you can easily generate thousands of assets doesn’t mean we’ll accept them on Adobe Stock. We’re looking for quality content that has unique value and strong potential for sales. Don’t submit multiple files with similar content. This can be perceived as spam and may result in your account being closed. Every image needs to have its own licensing potential.

Remember that Adobe Stock customers expect the best. They don’t want images of people with extra fingers or animals with extra legs.

## 5. Edit to Enhance Your Images

Adobe Stock customers often want space to add text or other graphics in their images, so you may want to add extra space if it doesn’t already exist. Consider sharpening, adjusting the exposure, and increasing vibrance, too. Do what’s necessary to enhance your images and make them look their best.

Adobe Stock artists are encouraged to use Adobe tools to make the most of your stock content. This includes generative fill powered by Firefly in Photoshop. Remember to label your image as generative AI if the use of generative fill changed, augmented, or added a new primary subject of an image. If you are using generative fill on a photograph only to extend a background or remove distracting objects, people, or intellectual property, then you do not need to label the image as generative AI.

## 6. Find the Right Size

Use a variety of sizes for your assets. In addition to the square files many generative AI software apps produce, consider customizing the aspect ratio to popular sizes such as 3:2 or 16:9 for more sales opportunities.

Also, increase the file size of your generated file to meet the Adobe Stock minimum file size requirement of 4 megapixels. Use the Enhance tool in Lightroom to increase the original file size, but don’t go beyond 6 to 8 megapixels.

## 7. Help Customers Find Your Content

You’re required to select the “Created using generative AI tools” checkbox in the Adobe Stock Contributor portal before submitting any content created entirely or partially with generative AI software. Failure to do so will result in file rejection.

After you upload an asset, you need to add a relevant title and keywords to describe the asset. This metadata is what surfaces your content in the search results.

Use words from your prompt in the title and repeat them in the keywords. Order the first 10 keywords by relevance (i.e., list the most important keywords first). Don’t include irrelevant keywords. Without good, relevant metadata, your content won’t be discovered by customers. Learn more about this important topic.

## 8. Is That Person Real?

If your asset features a person or property who is completely fictional and created 100% by generative AI software, check the box “I certify that people or property in this asset are not based on a recognizable person or property” in the Contributor portal in the Uploaded File section.

Is your generative AI asset based on a real living person or a real property? In this case, you must submit a signed model release by that person or a property release together with your generative AI asset. You can't generate an image featuring a celebrity and sell it for commercial use.

## 9. How Do You Make Money?

Every time a customer purchases a license to use your content, you earn a royalty. Ensure that you’re creating assets customers are looking for. Study trends by looking at how stock is used in the world around you, and then create content based on those trends.

Keep in mind that general categories in high demand include:

- Backgrounds
- Seasonal content related to major holidays like Christmas and Valentine’s Day
- Conceptual images that are time-consuming to produce with software like Photoshop and Illustrator
- Lifestyle images that include diverse people
- Images of technology, food, and nature
